    About KYLA

    Korea Youth Lacrosse Association (KYLA)Founded in 2026 to spread and develop youth lacrosse in Korea.

    A safe, enjoyable way into lacrosse for children

    The Korea Youth Lacrosse Association (KYLA) works to create an environment where children and teenagers can experience lacrosse safely and enjoyably, and grow through sport. We run youth education with schools and clubs, hold tournaments and exchange games, train coaches, and promote youth sports exchange in Korea and abroad.

    We do not believe the value of sport lies only in winning or in individual skill. Running with friends, taking on new challenges, finding your role in a team and respecting one another are, in themselves, what makes youth sport valuable.

    Why a youth organization was needed

    Lacrosse in Korea has grown around universities, adult clubs and the national teams. There were comparatively few places where children and teenagers could first meet the sport and keep playing. KYLA started from the view that the youth level needs four things.

    • A competition structure matched to age and level
    • Safety management centred on protecting players
    • Coach education and quality control of games
    • Transparent administration that parents can trust

    Mission and vision

    Mission

    To create an environment where young people can experience lacrosse safely and enjoyably, grow through sport, and keep taking part.

    Vision

    To build a sustainable youth sports ecosystem that connects schools, clubs, coaches, players and parents.

    Operating principles

    Safety first
    The physical and emotional safety of young players is the first criterion in every operating decision. We build field safety, protective equipment rules, emergency response and coach responsibility training into our systems.
    Education at the centre
    Youth games should be about growth more than results. Our guidelines set out a culture of sportsmanship, teamwork and respect.
    Transparent administration
    We manage every step openly, from team registration, game schedules and referee assignments to budgets and results reports.

    Youth programs

    Lacrosse that starts with fun and grows togetherPrograms matched to age and experience help children take on challenges for themselves, learn new skills and discover the joy of team sport.

    What kind of sport is lacrosse?

    Lacrosse is a team sport in which players use a stick with a net at the end to pass, carry and shoot a ball into the opposing goal. It has a long history that began in Indigenous communities of North America, and has spread around the world through schools, universities and clubs. It has been added to the Los Angeles 2028 Olympic programme in the 'sixes' format, with six players a side.

    Formats include field lacrosse, women's field lacrosse, sixes and box lacrosse, which is played indoors. At the youth entry level, games are played without contact and with modified rules.

    What it shares with other sports

    SportMovements you meet in lacrosse
    SoccerUse of space, movement off the ball, team tactics
    BasketballPasses and cuts, screens, transitions between attack and defence
    Ice hockeyStick control, fast attacks, shooting
    BaseballHand-eye coordination for catching and throwing accurately
    HandballAttacking the goal, building play with passes, shot selection

    What children experience

    Hand-eye coordination
    They repeatedly watch a moving ball and bring the stick to meet it.
    An eye for space
    They read where teammates and opponents are, find open space, and choose when to pass and move.
    Cooperation and communication
    They share roles, connect with teammates and respect opponents.
    Agility and body control
    They handle the stick while running, changing direction and keeping balance.
    The confidence to try for themselves
    They try, fail and adjust, learning to read the game for themselves.
    My place in the team
    They discover their own role and contribution within a team.

    By age group

    U8·U9
    An introduction through play. Children enjoy handling the stick and learn simple rules and safety.
    U10
    Passing, catching, cradling and moving. Small-sided team games build positive experiences.
    U12
    Ground balls, basic shooting, defensive positioning and use of space. Players experience real games.
    U14
    Applying skills accurately, switching between attack and defence, basic tactics and decision-making.
    U16
    Position-specific roles, more complex tactics and games, exchange in Korea and abroad, and the next step.

    Age groups are a guide for designing programs. The age and gender divisions of each tournament follow that tournament's guidelines.

    From a first try to tournaments and exchange

    1. Taster lessonPick up a stick for the first time.
    2. Regular after-school program or school clubLearn steadily every week.
    3. Friendly games within and between schoolsMeet other teams.
    4. KYLA youth eventsPlay in the Festival and the Championship.
    5. Camps and international exchangeGo on to wider experiences.

    Not every child needs to take the same path. We value playing for enjoyment over many years just as much as growing into a competitive player.

    Safety and coaching philosophy

    • Entry-level games are non-contact in principle, and use safe beginner sticks and protective equipment.
    • We do not group players by age alone. An older child who has just started may be less skilled than a younger child with experience, so we also look at actual experience and skill level.
    • We do not simply scale down adult rules. Games are run under our own rules designed for young players. Detailed rules are given in each tournament's guidelines.
    • We step in immediately when behaviour is dangerous, and practise safe movements repeatedly.

    A coach is not someone who pushes children to improve as fast as possible, but someone who sets the stage so that children enjoy sport and take part of their own accord.

    Putting enjoyment first does not mean lessons without preparation. Coaches plan lessons in advance, set safety rules and standards of behaviour, and design activities that fit each level. As much as how fast children improve, we care about whether they want to come back to the field.

    Coach education

    Education that raises the expertise and practical ability of youth coaches'Youth Lacrosse Coach', managed by KYLA, is a registered private qualification under the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism. We are preparing the General grade first.

    Qualification registration details

    Consumer notice

    For details of the registration and accreditation system for private qualifications, see the 'About private qualifications' section of the Private Qualification Information Service (www.pqi.or.kr, in Korean).

    Five written subjects for the General grade

    The exam does not only ask about technique and rules. It also covers the ability to design lessons and coach children safely.

    Lacrosse fundamentals

    • Types and structure of sticks, grip, safe use
    • Cradling, basic passing and catching, ground balls, shooting
    • Ball control on the move, skill demonstration, error correction

    Lacrosse rules

    • Game formats and team size, timing, starts and restarts, scoring, substitutions
    • Common fouls and penalties, modified youth rules
    • Basic officiating and safety-related calls

    Lacrosse tactics and game management

    • Basic principles of attack and defence, passing and movement
    • Occupying space, one-on-one attack and defence, transition situations
    • Position roles, communication and decision-making during games

    Designing youth training programs

    • Approaches by developmental stage, lesson objectives
    • Lesson structure, drills, games, feedback
    • Running mixed-level sessions for beginners and experienced players, motivation

    Sports safety and injury prevention

    • Checking equipment and facilities, safe stick use
    • Physical and emotional protection of young players, controlling risky behaviour
    • Injury prevention and basic emergency response, the coach's duty of care

    Practical assessment areas

    We do not look at skill demonstration alone. We assess whether you can explain in a way children understand, structure a lesson and run a session safely.

    • Basic passing and catching
    • Shooting and ball control
    • Tactical understanding and demonstration
    • Running a training program and explaining as a coach
    • Safety management and coaching manner

    Detailed criteria such as course hours, number of questions and pass marks follow the registered management and operating rules. They will be announced with the call for applicants.

    About KYLA

    The Korea Youth Lacrosse Association (KYLA) was founded in 2026 to give children and teenagers in Korea a safe and enjoyable way into lacrosse. We run youth lacrosse education with schools and clubs, organize youth tournaments, and train youth coaches.

    In 2026 we held the 1st Youth Lacrosse Festival on May 31 and the 1st KYLA Youth Lacrosse Championship on October 25.

    Based in Seoul, we link Seoul, Jeju and partners abroad, and want to create opportunities for schools, clubs and coaches in Korea and overseas to exchange and grow together through lacrosse.

    Ways to work together

    Our exchange opportunities are not limited to one tournament or a fixed schedule. Dates, length and format are agreed with each team.

    Visiting Korea
    Friendly games, joint training and cultural exchange with Korean youth teams in Seoul or Jeju.
    Hosting a Korean team
    Tournaments, games with local schools and clubs, and training visits in your country.
    Joint training
    Players and coaches from different countries training side by side.
    School and club matching
    We pair teams by level, age group and preferred season.
    International camps
    Age-group clinics and shared programs.
    Co-hosted events
    Friendship series or tournaments shaped by what both sides need.
    Coach exchange
    Sharing teaching methods, safety practice and culture.

    Privacy policy

    This is a draft. It will be published with an effective date once the association has confirmed retention periods, the privacy officer and contact details.

    Information we collect and why

    • Inquiries: name, organization, contact details and the content of the inquiry. Used to check and answer the inquiry.
    • Event entries: team name, school or club, head coach's name and contact details, and players' names, birth years and jersey numbers. Used to confirm entries, form divisions, run the event and manage safety.
    • International exchange inquiries: school or team name, country and city, contact person's name and contact details, and the exchange you have in mind. Used to discuss the exchange.
    • Expressions of interest: organization or team name, country and region, contact person's name, position and contact details, possible divisions and expected numbers, and preferred dates. Used to discuss and send information about that event.

    Retention period

    Information is destroyed without delay once the purpose of collection has been fulfilled. Retention periods for each item will be listed here once the association has confirmed them.

    Children under 14

    Personal information of children under 14 is collected with the consent of their legal guardian. Event entries are submitted by the team's head coach after confirming the consent of each player's guardian.

    Photos and video

    Photos from events and training sessions are posted after guardian consent has been confirmed. If you would like a posted photo removed, let us know through the inquiry form.

    Access, correction and deletion

    You or your legal guardian may ask to view, correct or delete your personal information. Please make the request through the inquiry form.
